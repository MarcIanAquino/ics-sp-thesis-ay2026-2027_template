# CMSC190 Special Project: Fiducial-Marker-Based Field Extraction for Mobile Digitization of Paper Evaluation Forms, with a Comparative Evaluation of On-Device Handwriting Recognition Engines
Authors: Marc Ian D.S. Aquino, Jaime P. Samaniego and Virma Rea G. Lee

Evaluation and satisfaction-survey forms collected during extension activities at the Institute of Plant Breeding, University of the Philippines Los Baños, are still encoded by hand, a slow and error-prone step that produces a backlog after every activity. This special problem redesigns the extraction pipeline of SEED (Scan, Encode, Export Data), a Flutter-based mobile application, around four solid fiducial markers printed at the corners of each form. A homography recovered from the detected markers warps every photograph into a fixed canonical page space in which each field occupies a pre-calibrated region. Likert-scale items are scored by Otsu-thresholded ink density, and only the handwritten fields are passed to a text-recognition engine, restricted to their own crops. Because field localization is decoupled from recognition, the recognizer is replaceable, and two engines can be compared on identical input. In an initial run on 106 completed IPB Evaluation Forms (English variant), 103 produced scored output, yielding 562 labelled handwritten-field crops. On single-line fields, a transformer-based handwriting recognizer (TrOCR) reduced the character error rate by 23.8 percent relative to Google ML Kit and raised exact-match agreement from 6.3 to 16.7 percent, while ML Kit returned no reading for 53.4 percent of all fields. This is the first of two parts; the evaluation across all three supported form types continues in Special Project 2.

Keywords: fiducial marker, homography, optical mark recognition, handwriting recognition, mobile document capture, form digitization
